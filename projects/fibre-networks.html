Master's thesis

Out-of-plane deformation and failure of 3D cellulose fibre networks

Master's thesis · Chair of Computational Applied Mechanics, University of Wuppertal · co-supervised by the Chair of Mechanics – Materials Theory, Ruhr University Bochum

AbaqusPython scriptingFibre networksCohesive interfacesMultiscaleFracture
PeriodAug 2026 – Feb 2027
ToolsAbaqus/Explicit, Python
MethodsFibre networks, cohesive interfaces, multiscale
ValidationExperimental results

Motivation

Cellulose-based materials such as paper and paperboard are increasingly used in sustainable technologies thanks to their renewable origin and biodegradability. Their in-plane mechanics have been studied extensively, but the out-of-plane response, which is important in forming processes, is much less understood.

Approach

  • Network generation: fibres are sampled from statistical distributions of length, width, wall thickness, microfibril angle and orientation, deposited layer by layer until a target grammage is reached, and then compacted in Abaqus/Explicit.
  • Mechanical analysis: the compacted geometry is imported stress-free; the bottom plate is clamped and the top plate is displaced through the thickness (out-of-plane tension) under displacement control.
  • Fibre bonds are modelled with cohesive interfaces, so that damage can be initiated either in the bonds or in the fibres.
  • Automation: the whole pipeline is scripted in Python, making parameter studies systematic and reproducible.

Research question

Is global damage driven by bond failure or by fibre failure, and how is damage propagated through the network? Microstructural cohesive properties are to be related to macroscopic quantities such as fracture toughness.
